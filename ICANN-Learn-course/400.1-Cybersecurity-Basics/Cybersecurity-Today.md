# Cybersecurity Today

> **Main idea:** Cybersecurity is no longer only an IT problem. It is a daily-life, business, and national-security issue because almost everything we do depends on connected devices, online accounts, cloud services, and digital data.

---

## 1. Today's Online Landscape

In the late 2010s, many cybersecurity lessons used Gartner's forecast that **20.4 billion connected devices** would be in use worldwide by 2020. That number helped show how quickly the Internet of Things (IoT) was growing.

Today, the message is even stronger: **the number of connected IoT devices continues to grow**, and each connected device can become a possible entry point for attackers.

| Topic | Around 2018 / old context | Updated context |
|---|---:|---:|
| Connected devices | Gartner forecasted **20.4 billion connected things by 2020** | IoT Analytics estimated **21.1 billion connected IoT devices by the end of 2025** |
| Digital dependency | Phones, social media, banking, smart homes, file sharing | Cloud work, remote access, AI tools, smart homes, healthcare devices, industrial systems, vehicles, and supply chains |
| Cyber risk | Hackers targeted personal and business information | Attackers also target **credentials, AI systems, third-party vendors, cloud platforms, and critical infrastructure** |

**Important:** The more connected devices and accounts we use, the larger the attack surface becomes.

---

## 2. Why Cybersecurity Matters

Most people now use digital systems for:

| Everyday activity | Why it matters for cybersecurity |
|---|---|
| Online banking | Financial data can be stolen or abused. |
| Email and messaging | Phishing and malware often begin here. |
| Social media | Attackers can impersonate people or collect personal information. |
| Smart home devices | Cameras, locks, routers, and assistants can expose private spaces. |
| Work accounts | One stolen password can give attackers access to company systems. |
| Cloud storage | Personal and confidential files can be leaked or encrypted by ransomware. |

**Cyber attacks take advantage of both human mistakes and system weaknesses.** A person may click a malicious link, use a weak password, or ignore an update; a system may have an unpatched vulnerability or poor configuration.

## 3. Cybersecurity Facts: 2018 Claims vs Current View

The original facts below were useful as a 2018-era awareness snapshot, but many numbers are now outdated. The table updates them with current, better-supported figures where possible.

| Cybersecurity fact | 2018-era statement | Updated view for 2025–2026 | What to remember |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Ransomware attacks** | Average demand around **$1,077** | Verizon DBIR 2025 reported ransomware in **44% of reviewed breaches**, and the **median ransom paid fell to $115,000**. | Ransomware is still common, but preparation and refusal to pay are improving outcomes. |
| **Data breaches** | Data breaches cost companies **$3.8 million** | IBM's 2025 report gives a **global average breach cost of $4.44 million**. | Breaches remain expensive even when average cost improves slightly. |
| **Email malware** | **92%** of malware delivered through email | Email remains a major threat channel, but modern attacks also use malicious URLs, QR codes, collaboration tools, SMS, and cloud accounts. | Do not treat attachments as the only danger; links and login prompts are often more dangerous. |
| **Email phishing** | **91%** of attacks begin with phishing email | Verizon DBIR 2025 shows phishing remains important, while vulnerability exploitation and stolen credentials are also major initial access vectors. | Phishing is still critical, but attackers now combine it with credential theft and exploited vulnerabilities. |
| **Identity theft** | **6.5%** of people are victims | FBI IC3 2024 reported **859,532 complaints** and **$16.6 billion in losses** from internet crime, with phishing/spoofing, extortion, and personal data breaches among top complaint types. | Identity theft is part of a larger fraud ecosystem. Protect personal data and accounts. |
| **Small business attacks** | **42%** of cyber attacks are in small businesses | Verizon's SMB snapshot says there were almost **four times more SMB victims than large-organization victims** in its dataset. | Small businesses are attractive because they often have weaker defenses. |
| **Every 39 seconds** | A hacker attack happens every 39 seconds | This statistic is widely repeated from older research, but it is not a precise current measurement. | The exact interval matters less than the lesson: attacks are continuous and automated. |
| **Records stolen daily** | Millions of records stolen every day since 2013 | Massive breaches still happen, but breach impact is now often measured by cost, credential exposure, ransomware, and supply-chain involvement. | Do not focus only on record counts; focus on what attackers can do with the data. |
| **Only 38% prepared** | Only **38%** of organizations feel prepared | Preparedness varies by threat. Recent reporting shows many organizations still feel underprepared for AI-enabled attacks and ransomware. | Readiness must be tested, not assumed. |
| **Resolve a malicious attack** | Average **50 days** to resolve | IBM 2025 notes breach cost decreased partly because identification and containment improved. | Faster detection and containment reduce damage. |
| **$16 billion stolen** | $16 billion stolen from 15.4 million U.S. consumers in 2016 | FBI IC3 2024 reported **$16.6 billion** in cyber-enabled losses in 2024. | Financial losses are still extremely high. |
| **Cybersecurity spending** | $1 trillion cumulatively from 2017–2021 | Cybersecurity Ventures projected **$454 billion annual global cybersecurity spending in 2025** and expects the market to continue growing. | Spending is growing because the digital attack surface is growing. |

## 4. What Is Cybersecurity?

**Cybersecurity** is the process of protecting digital information, online accounts, networks, devices, applications, and systems from unauthorized access, damage, theft, or disruption.

It includes three main activities:

| Activity | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Detect** | Identify suspicious activity or attacks. | An alert shows unusual login attempts from another country. |
| **Prevent** | Reduce the chance of an attack succeeding. | Multi-factor authentication blocks a stolen password. |
| **Respond** | Limit damage and recover after an incident. | A company isolates infected devices and restores backups. |

> **Key phrase:** Cybersecurity is not only about technology. It is also about people, processes, and habits.

## 5. Common Cyber Threat Actors

Different attackers have different goals. Understanding their motivation helps us understand the type of threat.

| Threat actor | Main motivation | Typical behavior | Example risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cyber criminals** | Profit | Steal data, sell credentials, run ransomware, commit fraud | Stolen bank login or ransomware demand |
| **Malicious hackers / black hats** | Profit, damage, reputation | Break into systems without permission | Database theft or website defacement |
| **Professional / ethical hackers / white hats** | Improve security | Test systems with permission | Penetration testing report |
| **Script kiddies** | Access, attention, disruption | Use ready-made tools without deep expertise | Defacing a website |
| **Worm and virus writers** | Notoriety, disruption, profit | Create malware that spreads | Malware infection across a network |
| **Security researchers** | Discovery, defense, sometimes profit | Find and responsibly report vulnerabilities | Bug bounty submission |
| **Hacktivists** | Political or social goals | Spread messages, leak data, disrupt services | Website defacement or data leak for a cause |
| **Cyber-terrorists** | Fear and disruption | Target public services or critical systems | Threats against infrastructure |
| **Nation-state / APT groups** | Espionage, long-term access, strategic advantage | Persistent attacks against governments, companies, and infrastructure | Stealing sensitive national or corporate information |
| **Industrial spies / organized crime groups** | Profit and competitive advantage | Steal trade secrets, blackmail organizations | Extortion using stolen business data |

**Important:** Not every hacker is a criminal. Some hackers work legally to find weaknesses before criminals exploit them.

## 6. Useful “Rewritten Phrases” for Notes or Presentation

| Original idea | Better academic wording |
|---|---|
| Hackers want to access information to commit crimes. | Cybercriminals attempt to gain unauthorized access to personal or proprietary data for financial gain, espionage, disruption, or fraud. |
| Cyber attacks spread like a disease. | Cyber attacks can propagate rapidly across connected systems, especially when networks are poorly segmented or devices remain unpatched. |
| People may not know they were hacked. | Many breaches remain undetected for days, weeks, or even months, allowing attackers to move laterally and expand their access. |
| Cybersecurity secures online accounts and devices. | Cybersecurity protects digital identities, devices, networks, applications, and data from unauthorized access, misuse, disruption, or destruction. |
| Small businesses are attacked too. | Small and medium-sized businesses are frequent targets because they often hold valuable data but may lack mature security controls. |

## 7. Basic Cybersecurity Habits Everyone Should Use

| Habit | Why it helps |
|---|---|
| **Use multi-factor authentication (MFA).** | A stolen password alone is not enough to log in. |
| **Use unique passwords with a password manager.** | One leaked password will not unlock every account. |
| **Think before clicking links.** | Phishing often starts with urgent or emotional messages. |
| **Update software and devices.** | Updates fix known vulnerabilities attackers may exploit. |
| **Back up important files.** | Backups help recover from ransomware or device failure. |
| **Limit what you share online.** | Less public information means fewer opportunities for impersonation. |
| **Check sender addresses and URLs carefully.** | Attackers often imitate trusted brands, banks, schools, or colleagues. |

> **Remember:** Security is strongest when good technology is combined with good habits.

## 8. Quick Knowledge Check

| Statement | Answer | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Hacktivists are politically or socially motivated. | **True** | Their attacks usually support a cause or agenda. |
| Cyber attacks take advantage of people and system vulnerabilities. | **True** | Attackers exploit both human behavior and technical weaknesses. |
| All hackers are criminals. | **False** | Ethical hackers help organizations improve security. |
| Email is the only cyber attack channel. | **False** | Attackers also use SMS, phone calls, malicious websites, cloud accounts, QR codes, and software vulnerabilities. |
| Small businesses do not need cybersecurity. | **False** | Small businesses are often targeted because their defenses may be weaker. |

---

## 9. Conclusions: What Is Important to Remember

- **Cybersecurity matters because our daily life depends on connected devices and online accounts.**
- **The attack surface is growing:** more devices, more cloud services, more identities, more third-party connections.
- **People are part of security.** Phishing, weak passwords, and careless clicks are still major risks.
- **Ransomware and data breaches remain expensive and common.**
- **Small businesses are not “too small” to be attacked.**
- **Fast detection, backups, MFA, updates, and security awareness reduce damage.**
